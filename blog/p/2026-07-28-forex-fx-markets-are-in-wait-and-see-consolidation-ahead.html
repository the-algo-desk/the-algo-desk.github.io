What the headlines actually say

FX markets are in wait-and-see consolidation ahead of FOMC and other central bank decisions this week. Yen and Aussie are mildly bid on repositioning, EUR/USD is drifting toward YTD lows near 1.1324, and the USD is testing major resistance — but nothing is breaking until the Fed speaks.

So what changes

For a small account trading FX into a central bank week, the honest read is: don't. Pre-FOMC FX ranges are where retail gets chopped by institutional repositioning and spread widening. The EUR/USD drift toward 1.1324 is the only semi-coherent trend, but it's a slow grind, not a breakout — and if USD resistance fails post-FOMC, the reversal is violent. Your edge here is patience: wait for the actual decision, let the first 30-minute spike resolve, and only act if there's a clean break of a pre-announced level with volume confirmation. Trading the anticipation is paying for optionality you don't need.

Unless the FOMC delivers a surprise outside expectations, this is just positioning noise—short any premature breakout with confidence.

Where they genuinely disagree

The bull reads risk-on repositioning (yen/Aussie strength, TWD defense) as genuine optimism; the bear reads the same as noise-trapping positioning that reverts post-FOMC. Both essentially agree the real catalyst hasn't arrived — the split is only about whether pre-positioning has directional follow-through.

What to watch next

FOMC statement and dot plot: does the Fed signal a September cut explicitly or stay ambiguous? A dovish surprise breaks USD resistance downward (EUR/USD through 1.1350); a hawkish hold accelerates EUR/USD toward 1.1324 and below. That single print settles whether the pre-positioning was directionally correct or trap.
